The argument in four steps

1. The decline is one customer, not a trend

US insurance units fell 7.5% in FY26 Q4. Management said that excluding one single customer loss, domestic insurance assignments would have risen 2.3%. Reporting and analyst commentary identify the customer as Progressive, which shifted from roughly 75% to roughly 90% of its salvage volume to IAA beginning around January 2026.

2. The comparison stops hurting in FY27 Q2

Because the shift began in January 2026, FY27 Q1 (August to October 2026) still compares against a pre-loss base and will probably print another decline of six to eight percent. The lap arrives in FY27 Q2 and completes in Q3. That is when the underlying two to three percent growth becomes the reported number. This is a correction to our own earlier framing, which had the inflection a quarter too early.

3. IAA bought the volume with price

RB Global's service revenue take rate fell 110 basis points year over year to 20.0% in Q2 2026. The company attributes this to acquisition mix and to “automotive pricing incentives tied to higher transaction volumes”. Copart declined to match: its chief executive said on the call, “We don't need to play that game. We've got amazing liquidity.”

4. Lower utilisation may explain part of the cost pressure

Operating cost per car rose 12.7% and US facility cost per unit rose 14.2%, against revenue per unit up 5.4%. Lower utilisation, fuel and wages may all contribute. The cost-decomposition study is not built, so the recoverable margin benefit is still a hypothesis.

Three reasons long, three reasons short

The judges asked for both sides argued honestly. Here they are.

Long Why this works

  1. A one-customer problem read as structural decay. It laps in FY27 Q2 to Q3 and the rest of the insurance book is already growing 2.3%.
  2. Operating leverage in reverse. Cost per car is inflated by under-utilisation and input inflation. Volume stabilisation could improve margin; the size of that benefit still needs to be modelled.
  3. Priced for the bear case. Near the bottom of a twenty-year multiple range, no debt, net cash, $1.63bn of buybacks in FY26, and a consensus that already assumes flat-to-negative US service revenue.

Short Why it might not

  1. The share loss may be structural. Six straight quarters of IAA outperformance, disclosed service-level metrics that insurers value, and its largest partner now covered in all 50 states. Barclays explicitly flags risk of further losses.
  2. Not all the cost inflation is cyclical. Gasoline CPI was up 27% year over year in August and diesel rose 36% in the quarter. If used-vehicle values roll over, the pricing offset that has held revenue flat disappears at the same time.
  3. Capital allocation and leadership changed. The founder-era chief executive returned in July, and $1.9bn of cash is going to ACV Auctions, a dealer-to-dealer marketplace outside core salvage that is only breakeven in FY27.
What would make us wrong, stated in advance

A second large carrier disclosed as lost in FY27 Q1. RB Global's consolidated take rate stabilising or rising while automotive volume growth stays above 8% would weaken our pricing-pressure thesis, subject to acquisition mix. Copart's cost per car still rising at a double-digit rate in FY27 Q1. Or the Manheim used-vehicle index falling below roughly 195, which removes the pricing offset.
