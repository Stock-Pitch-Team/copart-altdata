## 1. Thesis in one paragraph

Copart's US insurance unit decline is almost entirely one customer. Fiscal Q4 US insurance units fell 7.5%, but management said that excluding "1 single customer loss" domestic insurance assignments would have **grown 2.3%**. That customer is widely reported to be Progressive, which moved from roughly 75% to roughly 90%+ of its salvage volume to IAA (RB Global) starting around January 2026. The Street is extrapolating a share-loss spiral (Barclays PT $26 at 10x EBITDA, HSBC downgrade to Hold on 17 Sep). We think the market is mispricing three things: (1) the Progressive loss laps in FY27 Q2–Q3, at which point the underlying +2–3% ex-Progressive growth becomes the reported number; (2) IAA is paying for its volume with pricing incentives (its take rate fell 110 bp YoY to 20.0% in Q2 2026), so its gains are lower-quality and its ability to keep bidding for share is finite; (3) Copart's cost base is fixed and currently under-utilized (opex per car +12.7% YoY in Q4), so any volume stabilization produces outsized margin recovery. At ~$29.6 the stock trades ~19x trailing EPS of $1.55, near the bottom of its 20-year 7x–25x EV/EBITDA range, with >$2B cash after the ACV deal closes, no debt, and $1.63B of buybacks executed in FY26.

## 4. The variant view, restated with correct timing

Your original framing ("FY27 Q1–Q2 insurance units tick above consensus because IAA can't sustain rebates") needs two adjustments.

1. **Timing.** The Progressive shift started ~Jan 2026 and completed over the following ~6 months. FY27 Q1 (Aug–Oct 2026) still comps against a pre-loss base, so reported US insurance units will likely print another −6% to −8%. The lap arrives in **FY27 Q2 (Nov–Jan)** and is complete by **FY27 Q3 (Feb–Apr)**. That is when the +2–3% ex-Progressive growth becomes the headline number. Pitch the inflection as Q2→Q3 FY27, and treat Q1 as the last bad print (and possibly the entry point).
2. **Mechanism.** Copart itself walked away from Progressive's pricing. Near-term recovery of *Progressive* volume requires either Copart conceding (unlikely under Adair's stated stance) or Progressive experiencing service failures at IAA. The durable version of the thesis is not "Progressive comes back." It is:
   - the rest of Copart's insurance book is growing (+2.3%) and carriers other than Progressive (GEICO, State Farm, Allstate, USAA) are re-growing policies after the 2022–23 retrenchment;
   - IAA's share gains are being bought with take-rate concessions (−110 bp), so IAA's incremental economics are worse than the headline volume suggests, which limits how many more contracts it can win on price;
   - Copart's ~10-point unit hole is now a fixed-cost utilization problem; every point of unit recovery falls through at very high incremental margin (Q4 gross margin 41.8% vs ~45% run-rate is the size of the prize);
   - pricing/RPU (+5.4%) and international (+10–12%) keep growing regardless.

At Cx FY28 EPS this implies D% upside against E% downside at Barclays' 10x EBITDA bear case. Next evidence: (1) Q1 FY27 print mid-Nov, watch ex-Progressive commentary and cost per car; (2) RBA Q3 print early Nov, watch automotive take rate; (3) Progressive monthly PIF; (4) our weekly inventory tracker."

## 5. Three reasons long, three reasons short (as the judges asked)

**Long**
1. A one-customer problem is being read as structural share loss; it laps in FY27 Q2–Q3 and the ex-Progressive book is already growing.
2. Negative operating leverage today (opex/car +12.7%) becomes positive operating leverage on stabilization; TLF at record and repair inflation keep the funnel growing.
3. Valuation at the bottom of its historical range, net cash, aggressive buyback, sentiment washed out (Barclays $26, HSBC downgrade), and a low consensus bar (flat-to-negative US service revenue).

**Short**
1. Share loss is structural: RBA has six straight quarters of outperformance, disclosed SLAs that insurers value, and its largest partner now in all 50 states; more RFPs may go the same way (Barclays flags "risk of additional share losses over the next few years"). Progressive is the fastest-growing large carrier, so the carrier-mix headwind compounds.
2. Cost inflation is not all cyclical: diesel, labor, and facility costs rose while volumes fell; if Manheim rolls over, ASP support disappears and revenue per unit stalls at the same time.
3. Capital allocation and leadership drift: CEO change back to the founder-era CEO, a $1.9B cash acquisition outside the core salvage business that is only breakeven in FY27, and less cash for buybacks; a below-normal hurricane season removes CAT optionality for this fiscal year.

**What would make us take the other side:** a second large-carrier loss disclosed in FY27 Q1; RBA automotive take rate stabilizing or rising while volume growth stays >8% (means incentives were not the driver); Copart cost per car still rising double digits in Q1 FY27; Manheim index below ~195.
